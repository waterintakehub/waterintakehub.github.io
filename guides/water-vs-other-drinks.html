Water vs Other Drinks: What Counts?

Which drinks hydrate you, which ones do not, and how tea, coffee, juice, milk and alcohol compare with water.

Not all drinks hydrate equally, and the old rules about which ones count are often wrong. Tea and coffee do count, despite their reputation. Milk hydrates well. Fruit juice hydrates but adds sugar. Alcohol does the opposite. This guide compares the common drinks honestly, so you can make good choices without giving up the drinks you enjoy.

Water: the standard

Plain water is the best drink for hydration: it has no calories, no sugar, no caffeine, and it is what the body uses directly. It is also the cheapest and most available. For most people, most of the time, water should be the default drink, with other drinks as additions rather than replacements. If you find plain water boring, add a slice of lemon, cucumber or mint, or drink sparkling water, which hydrates just as well as still water.

Tea and coffee

Tea and coffee do count towards fluid intake. The idea that they dehydrate you comes from caffeine's mild diuretic effect, but the water in the drink more than compensates for it at normal intakes. A cup of tea or coffee contributes almost as much fluid as a cup of water. The difference is what you add: sugar and syrups add calories, and very large amounts of caffeine can disturb sleep. Two to four cups a day is fine for most healthy adults, and they can be part of your daily fluid total.

Milk and milky drinks

Milk hydrates well, and it also provides protein, calcium and other nutrients, which makes it a useful drink, especially for children and older adults. Its calories count, though: a large glass of whole milk is about 150 calories. Flavoured milks and milkshakes add sugar on top. Milk is a food as well as a drink, so it is best counted as part of a meal rather than as a replacement for water.

Juice and sugary drinks

Fruit juice contains water and vitamins, but also a lot of natural sugar: a glass of orange juice has about the same sugar as a fizzy drink, without the fibre of the whole fruit. Fizzy drinks, energy drinks and sweetened teas add sugar and calories without nutrients. These drinks do hydrate, but they make weight management harder, and they are bad for teeth. If you drink them, keep them occasional and in small glasses, and choose water as the everyday drink.

Alcohol

Alcohol is the one common drink that does not hydrate: it suppresses the hormone that helps the kidneys retain water, so you lose more water than you drink. That is why a night of drinking leaves you dehydrated the next morning. Alcohol also adds calories, about 7 per gram, and disturbs sleep. If you drink, alternate each alcoholic drink with a glass of water, and drink water before bed. The best choice for hydration is to keep alcohol moderate and separate from your fluid target.

A day of drinks, compared

Take a day with three mugs of tea, a glass of milk, a glass of juice and a litre of water. The tea contributes about 750 ml of fluid, the milk about 200 ml, the juice about 200 ml, and the water 1,000 ml, for a total of about 2,150 ml of fluid, plus the water in food. The tea and water are the best choices: they hydrate without calories. The milk hydrates and adds nutrients, but also about 150 calories. The juice hydrates but adds about 100 calories of sugar. If the juice were swapped for water, the day would have the same fluid and 100 fewer calories. The comparison shows that the choice is rarely about hydration, which most drinks provide, but about the calories and sugar that come with it.

Tip: Tea and coffee do count towards your fluid intake — the water in the drink more than makes up for caffeine's mild diuretic effect at normal intakes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do tea and coffee dehydrate you?

No, not at normal intakes. They count towards fluid intake almost as much as water does.

Is milk good for hydration?

Yes, milk hydrates well and adds protein and calcium, but it also has calories, so treat it as part of a meal.

Does alcohol hydrate you?

No. Alcohol increases water loss, so you lose more than you drink. Alternate alcoholic drinks with water.

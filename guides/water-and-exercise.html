Water and Exercise: How Much Extra Do You Need?

How sweat losses change with exercise, how to replace them before, during and after training, and when salts matter.

Exercise raises water needs sharply, because sweat is the body's cooling system and a hard session can cost a litre or more of fluid an hour. Replacing that fluid well matters: even mild dehydration makes exercise feel harder and raises heart rate. This guide explains how much you lose, how to replace it, and when plain water is not enough.

How much do you sweat

Sweat rates vary enormously. A light session in a cool room might cost 300 to 500 ml an hour, while a hard session in heat can cost 1 to 2 litres an hour. Body size matters: larger people sweat more. Fitness matters too, because trained people start sweating earlier and sweat more. Humidity matters most of all: in humid air, sweat evaporates poorly, so the body produces more of it. The only way to know your own rate is to weigh yourself before and after a session: each kilogram lost is roughly a litre of fluid.

Before exercise

Drink about 300 to 500 ml of water in the two hours before exercise, so you start hydrated rather than trying to catch up. Avoid drinking a large volume all at once just before starting, because it can sit uncomfortably. If you are training first thing in the morning, drink a glass on waking, because you will have lost water overnight. A pale urine colour before you start is a good sign that you are ready.

During exercise

For sessions under an hour, sipping water regularly is usually enough: small amounts every 15 to 20 minutes beat large gulps rarely. For sessions over an hour, plan to drink 400 to 800 ml per hour, adjusted for heat and how much you sweat. In hot conditions, drink before you feel thirsty, because thirst lags behind loss during exercise. Carry water with you for outdoor sessions, and know where you can refill on a long run or ride.

After exercise

Replace the fluid you lost, plus a little extra: a common guideline is to drink about 1.5 litres for every kilogram of body weight lost during the session, spread over the hours after exercise rather than all at once. Food helps too, because meals contain water and salt, which helps the body hold on to the fluid. A salty snack or a meal after a long, sweaty session replaces the salts lost in sweat better than water alone.

When water alone is not enough

For sessions longer than about 90 minutes, or in very hot conditions, you lose significant salts as well as water, and replacing water alone can dilute the blood. Sports drinks, or water plus a salty snack, replace both. Ordinary meals after exercise usually cover the salts for shorter sessions. Watch for warning signs of serious dehydration or heat illness: dizziness, confusion, very dark urine, or no sweating despite heat. These need prompt cooling and fluids, and medical attention if they do not improve quickly.

A training week, added up

Take a person who trains five times a week for an hour, losing about 700 ml of sweat per session. That is 3,500 ml of extra water a week, or 500 ml a day on average, on top of the base target. On a training day, the extra is 700 ml, and on a rest day it is nothing, which is why a single daily target does not fit a training week. The practical fix is two targets: a rest-day target from the main calculator, and a training-day target that adds the session's sweat loss. Write both down, and use the training-day target on training days. The extra water is best spread around the session: 300 to 500 ml before, sips during, and the rest afterwards with food, which replaces the salts as well.

Tip: Weigh yourself before and after a session to find your sweat rate — each kilogram lost is roughly a litre of fluid to replace.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much water do I need when exercising?

Roughly 400 to 800 ml per hour during exercise, plus 300 to 500 ml before and replacement afterwards. It varies with heat and sweat rate.

Should I drink during a short workout?

For sessions under an hour, sipping water is usually enough. Drink before you start and replace fluids afterwards.

When do I need a sports drink instead of water?

For sessions over about 90 minutes, or in very hot conditions, when you lose significant salts as well as water.

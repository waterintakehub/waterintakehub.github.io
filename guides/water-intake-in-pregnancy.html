Water Intake in Pregnancy

How much water to drink during pregnancy, why needs rise, and how to stay hydrated safely.

Pregnancy raises the body's need for water, because blood volume increases, the baby is surrounded by amniotic fluid, and the body is supporting two sets of needs. Most pregnant people need more water than they did before, and the increase is modest but real. This guide explains how much more, why it matters, and how to stay hydrated comfortably through each trimester.

How much more water do you need

Pregnancy raises total water needs by roughly 300 ml a day, on top of the usual weight-based figure, and breastfeeding raises them further, by roughly 500 to 700 ml a day. For a 65 kg pregnant person, the weight-based figure is about 2,000 to 2,300 ml, plus about 300 ml, giving roughly 2,300 to 2,600 ml of total water a day, including food. These are guides for healthy pregnancies, not prescriptions, and your doctor or midwife can give a personal figure, especially if you have a health condition.

Why water matters in pregnancy

Water supports the increased blood volume of pregnancy, helps carry nutrients to the baby, and forms the amniotic fluid that cushions and protects the baby. Good hydration also helps with common pregnancy complaints: it can ease constipation, reduce headaches, and lower the risk of urinary infections, which are more common in pregnancy. Dehydration in pregnancy can cause dizziness, headaches and contractions, so it is worth taking seriously, especially in hot weather or when you are unwell.

Morning sickness and hydration

Nausea and vomiting in early pregnancy make drinking difficult, and they also increase water loss, which is a difficult combination. Small, frequent sips are easier than large glasses, and cold water, ice chips, or water with a slice of lemon or ginger can be easier to keep down. If you cannot keep any fluids down for a day, or you are vomiting repeatedly, seek medical advice promptly, because dehydration in pregnancy can develop quickly and may need treatment.

Practical tips for staying hydrated

Keep a water bottle with you at all times, and sip regularly rather than waiting until you are thirsty. Drink a glass with every meal and snack, and keep one by your bed for the night. Eat water-rich foods such as fruit, vegetables, soups and yoghurt, which add fluid without effort. Avoid alcohol entirely in pregnancy, and keep caffeine moderate, as your doctor advises. If your urine is pale straw and you feel well, your intake is probably right.

When to seek advice

See a doctor or midwife promptly if you cannot keep fluids down, if you have had no urine for eight hours, if you feel dizzy or confused, or if you notice a sharp drop in how often you urinate. Swelling of the hands and face, severe headaches, or visual changes in pregnancy need prompt medical attention, because they can signal conditions that need care. Hydration is one part of a healthy pregnancy, and it is worth discussing with your midwife at your regular appointments.

A day of drinking in pregnancy

A practical day for a pregnant person might look like this. A glass of water on waking, a glass with breakfast, small sips through the morning, a glass with lunch, a glass in the afternoon, a glass with dinner, and a small glass in the evening, for a total of about 2 to 2.5 litres of drinks, plus the water in food. Keep a bottle with you at all times, and sip little and often rather than drinking large volumes at once, which can feel uncomfortable. If nausea makes drinking hard, try ice chips, cold water with lemon, or small sips every few minutes. The routine is gentle and regular, and it keeps hydration steady without ever feeling like a chore.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much water should I drink in pregnancy?

About 300 ml a day more than your usual weight-based figure, roughly 2,300 to 2,600 ml of total water for a 65 kg person. Your doctor or midwife can give a personal figure.

Does morning sickness cause dehydration?

It can, because vomiting increases water loss while making drinking difficult. Small, frequent sips help, and seek advice if you cannot keep fluids down for a day.

Should I drink more while breastfeeding?

Yes, by roughly 500 to 700 ml a day more than usual, because producing milk uses water. Keep a bottle nearby while feeding.

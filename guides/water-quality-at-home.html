Water Quality at Home

How tap water is treated, when a filter helps, and how to store water safely at home.

Tap water in most developed countries is treated and tested to strict standards, and it is safe to drink straight from the tap. Yet many people worry about its taste, its hardness, or its contents, and many buy filters or bottled water. This guide explains how tap water is treated, when a filter is useful, and how to store water safely at home.

How tap water is treated

Tap water is treated at a plant before it reaches your home. The treatment usually includes filtering to remove particles, disinfection with chlorine or a similar agent to kill germs, and testing for bacteria, chemicals and metals. The water company must test the water regularly and publish the results, which are usually available on its website. The chlorine taste some people notice is a sign the disinfection is working, and it fades if the water is left in an open jug in the fridge for a few hours.

When a filter helps

A filter can improve taste and remove some substances, and it is worth considering if your water tastes strongly of chlorine, if it is very hard and leaves scale, or if your pipes are old and may contain lead. A simple jug filter with activated carbon improves taste and removes some chlorine and metals. A more advanced filter can remove more, but it also costs more and needs regular cartridge changes. A filter that is not maintained can become a source of bacteria, so follow the manufacturer's replacement schedule.

Hard and soft water

Hard water contains more calcium and magnesium, which leave scale on kettles and taps but are not a health risk; in fact, they contribute small amounts of those minerals to your diet. Soft water has fewer minerals and does not leave scale, but it can taste flat. Neither is better for hydration, and the choice of a softener is about appliances and taste rather than health. If you have very hard water, a filter or softener is a convenience, not a necessity.

Bottled water vs tap water

Bottled water is convenient when you are out, but at home it is rarely better than tap water, and it costs far more and creates plastic waste. In many places, bottled water and tap water come from similar sources and meet similar standards. A reusable bottle filled from the tap is cheaper, better for the environment, and just as safe. If you prefer the taste of bottled water, a jug filter gives a similar result at a fraction of the cost.

Storing water safely

Store water in a clean, covered container in the fridge, and use it within a day or two. Wash reusable bottles daily with hot soapy water, and let them dry fully, because a damp bottle left for days can grow bacteria. Do not store water in direct sunlight, and do not reuse single-use plastic bottles for long periods, because they are hard to clean and can crack. If your water ever smells unusual, looks cloudy, or tastes strongly chemical, contact your water company and use bottled water until it is checked.

Checking your own tap water

You can check your tap water in three ways. First, look at it: it should be clear, with no colour or particles. Second, smell it: a faint chlorine smell is normal and fades in an open jug, but a strong chemical or musty smell is worth reporting. Third, read your water company's report, which is published on its website and lists the test results for bacteria, chemicals and metals in your area. If the water is cloudy, smells unusual, or tastes strongly chemical, contact the company and use bottled water until it is checked. For most people, most of the time, the tap is fine, and a jug filter is a matter of taste rather than safety.

Tip: Tap water is treated and tested to strict standards in most developed countries — a jug filter improves taste, but it is a convenience, not a necessity for safety.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is tap water safe to drink?

In most developed countries, yes. It is treated, disinfected and tested regularly to strict standards.

Do I need a water filter?

Not for safety, usually. A filter improves taste and can help with very hard water or old pipes, but it must be maintained.

Is bottled water better than tap water?

Rarely. At home, a reusable bottle filled from the tap is cheaper, safer for the environment, and just as safe.

Your Daily Hydration Schedule

How to spread your water intake across the day, from the first glass in the morning to the last one before bed.

Drinking enough water is less about willpower than about timing. A day's target spread across eight or nine small drinks is easy; the same amount attempted in two or three large drinks is uncomfortable and mostly ends up in the toilet. This guide builds a practical schedule around a normal day, from waking to bedtime.

Start the day with a glass

You lose water overnight through breathing and sweating, and you wake up mildly dehydrated. A glass of water on waking, about 250 ml, replaces some of that loss and starts the day well. Keep a glass or bottle by your bed or in the bathroom so the habit is easy. Coffee or tea afterwards counts towards fluids too, but a glass of water first is a simple anchor for the day.

Tie drinks to meals

Meals are the natural anchors of a hydration schedule. A glass with breakfast, one with lunch, and one with dinner adds about 750 ml without any extra effort, and drinking with meals helps digestion. Add a glass mid-morning and one mid-afternoon, and you are at five glasses before the evening. The pattern is easy to remember because it follows the pattern of the day, and it spreads intake evenly rather than bunching it.

Keep water within reach

The single most effective hydration habit is keeping water visible and close. A bottle on your desk, in your bag, or in the car means you drink without thinking about it, while water in another room means you forget. Refill the bottle when it is empty rather than waiting until you feel thirsty. Many people find that switching from small glasses to a 750 ml or 1 litre bottle helps, because the target becomes a number of bottles rather than a number of glasses.

Taper off before bed

Drink less in the last hour or two before bed, so you are not waking up at night to use the toilet. A small sip if you are thirsty is fine, but a large glass late in the evening usually costs sleep. If you wake up thirsty at night, keep a small glass by the bed rather than drinking a lot. The bulk of your intake should happen in the morning and afternoon, when your body uses it and your kidneys can process it comfortably.

Adjust the schedule to your day

A schedule should fit your life, not fight it. If you work outdoors in heat, drink more in the morning and carry extra water. If you exercise in the evening, add drinks around the session. If you forget, set a reminder on your phone for mid-morning and mid-afternoon, the two times people most often miss. Use the hydration schedule planner on this site to build a timetable from your wake and bed times, and adjust it over a week until it feels natural.

A schedule for a 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. day

Take a day that runs from 7 a.m. to 11 p.m., sixteen hours awake, with a target of 2,500 ml. Spread across eight drinks, that is about 300 ml each: 7 a.m. on waking, 9 a.m. mid-morning, 11 a.m. before lunch, 1 p.m. with lunch, 3 p.m. mid-afternoon, 5 p.m. before dinner, 7 p.m. with dinner, and 9 p.m. in the evening. The last drink is small, so it does not disturb sleep. If the day is hot or includes training, add a drink at 12 p.m. and one at 4 p.m., and the total rises to about 3,000 ml without any drink being large. The schedule works because it is tied to the day's natural breaks, and each drink is small enough to be comfortable.

Tip: Tie drinks to meals: a glass with breakfast, lunch and dinner plus one mid-morning and one mid-afternoon gets you to five glasses before the evening.

Frequently Asked Questions

How should I spread water through the day?

In small drinks across the day: a glass on waking, one with each meal, and one mid-morning and mid-afternoon, tapering off before bed.

Should I drink water before bed?

A small sip is fine, but avoid a large glass in the last hour or two, or you may wake up at night.

Does a bottle on my desk really help?

Yes. Keeping water visible and within reach is the most effective hydration habit, because you drink without having to remember.
